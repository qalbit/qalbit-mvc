ERP pricing is deliberately murky. Packaged vendors quote per-user licences that balloon at renewal; big integrators quote "it depends" until you've sat through three sales calls. ## Quick answer: custom ERP cost in 2026

A **phase-one custom ERP** — typically inventory, purchase and sales for one company — costs **$20,000–$42,000** with an experienced India-based team. Full multi-module systems with production, finance integration and HR land at **$42,000–$75,000** built out over phases. Enterprise-suite implementations (SAP, Oracle) start around $150,000 and routinely pass $500,000 — a different universe.

## Why ERP costs vary 4× between quotes

1. **Module count and depth.** "Inventory" can mean one warehouse with simple stock counts, or multi-location batches, serial numbers, barcode scanning and stock audits. Scope each module in writing before comparing quotes.
2. **Master data reality.** ERPs run on clean masters — items, parties, BOMs, locations. If your current data lives in inconsistent spreadsheets, budget real time for cleaning and migration. This is the most commonly hidden cost in ERP quotes.
3. **Integration strategy.** The cheapest reliable path for most SMEs: keep the accounting tool your CA already trusts (Tally, Zoho Books, QuickBooks) and sync with it, rather than rebuilding accounting. Rebuilding compliance-grade accounting adds $15,000+ and risk.
4. **Parallel run vs big bang.** A parallel run — old tools and new ERP side by side until numbers reconcile — adds 3–6 weeks of support cost and is worth every dollar. Big-bang cutovers are where ERP horror stories come from.
5. **Who builds it.** The same phase-one scope quoted at $25,000 by an India-based senior team is a $70,000–$100,000 project at US integrator rates.

## The phased rollout that protects your budget

ERP projects fail when they try to change everything at once. The sequence we use on every [custom ERP development](https://qalbit.com/services/erp-development/) engagement:

1. **Discovery (2–3 weeks).** Map order-to-cash and procure-to-pay as they actually run — including the WhatsApp messages and Excel sheets. Identify the highest-pain module.
2. **Phase one build (6–14 weeks).** Usually inventory + purchase + sales. Weekly demos with your real items and parties, not dummy data.
3. **Migration & parallel run (3–6 weeks).** Masters and opening balances migrated; supervisors trained; old system keeps running until daily numbers match.
4. **Next modules, on evidence.** Production, finance sync, HR — each phase funded only after the previous one has proven itself on the floor.

Each phase is a fixed-scope, fixed-budget commitment. You never sign up for the whole number on day one.

## Custom ERP vs packaged ERP costs

Odoo, SAP Business One and similar packages look cheaper upfront — but per-user licences, mandatory partners for customisation, and forced upgrades change the five-year math. And customising a package beyond ~30% of its defaults usually costs more than building the modules you need. The full comparison is in our guide: [custom ERP vs Odoo vs SAP](https://qalbit.com/blog/custom-erp-vs-odoo-vs-sap/).

The one-line version: **if your processes are standard, packaged wins; if your processes are your advantage, custom wins.**

## FAQ

**How long does a custom ERP take to implement?**
Phase one takes 3–5 months including discovery, build, migration, training and a parallel run. Full multi-module builds spread over 9–18 months — deliberately, so operations never stop.

**Can we keep Tally / our accountant's software?**
Yes, and for most SMEs you should. Two-way sync (vouchers, invoices, payments, GST/e-invoicing flows) costs far less than rebuilding accounting and keeps your CA in familiar territory.

**What are the ongoing costs after go-live?**
Hosting plus a support retainer — typically 10–15% of build cost per year for fixes, reports and small enhancements. There are no per-user licences on a custom ERP, which is exactly why the math improves as you grow.

**Is $20,000 really enough for an ERP?**
For a focused phase one at India-based rates, yes — we've shipped them. It's not enough for 14 modules at once, and any vendor who says otherwise is planning change requests.
