## What you're actually paying for

A custom CRM isn't one thing — it's a set of modules. Understanding the module costs makes every agency quote easier to evaluate:

**Core modules (always included):**
- **Contacts & accounts (customer 360)** — every interaction, deal, document and invoice against one customer record.
- **Lead capture & pipeline** — web forms, stages, assignment rules, follow-up reminders.
- **User roles & permissions** — who sees which leads, territories and reports.

**Common additions (each adds 5–12% to the budget):**
- **Quoting & approvals** — quote builder connected to your price list, with approval flows.
- **Dashboards & reporting** — conversion funnels, pipeline value, activity leaderboards.
- **Third-party integrations** — telephony (click-to-call), WhatsApp/email capture, accounting sync. Integrations are the single most common budget-mover.
- **Automation** — lead scoring, stale-deal alerts, renewal reminders.
- **Mobile companion app** — for field sales teams.

## The five factors that move the price

1. **Number of workflows, not number of users.** Custom CRM pricing is driven by how many distinct processes you need (sales, service, renewals, field visits), not headcount. That's the opposite of SaaS licensing — and why custom wins as teams grow.
2. **Integration count.** Each external system — ERP, Tally/QuickBooks, telephony, marketing tools — adds design, build and testing time. Three integrations can add $4,000–$8,000.
3. **Data migration.** Moving five years of messy spreadsheet data (deduplication, history preservation, verification) is real work. Budget $1,500–$5,000 depending on volume and quality.
4. **Vertical complexity.** A real-estate CRM with property inventory and site-visit scheduling, or a healthcare CRM with patient journeys, costs more than a generic pipeline — but it's also exactly where custom development beats configuring a generic tool.
5. **Who builds it.** A Philadelphia or London agency bills $120–$220/hour. A senior India-based team like QalbIT delivers the same stack (Laravel/Node.js + React) at a fraction of that, with US/UK/GCC time-zone overlap.

The comparison that actually matters isn't "custom vs free" — it's custom vs three years of per-seat fees:

- A $20,000 custom CRM is **paid for once**. Year two and three cost only hosting and a modest support retainer — and it fits your process exactly.

## Where teams overspend (and how to avoid it)

- **Building all modules at once.** Launch with leads + pipeline + customer 360; add quoting and automation after your team has adopted the core. Phased rollouts cut first-release cost by 30–40%.
- **Rebuilding what integration solves.** Don't rebuild accounting inside your CRM — sync with Tally, Zoho Books or QuickBooks instead. The same goes for [CRM–ERP integration](https://qalbit.com/services/erp-development/): connect systems, don't duplicate them.
- **Custom design where patterns work.** Sales teams want fast, keyboard-friendly screens, not award-winning visuals. Proven UI patterns save 10–15%.

## FAQ

**How long does custom CRM development take?**
A usable first release takes 9–14 weeks: 1–2 weeks of process mapping, 1–3 weeks of design, then weekly build increments. Teams typically go live module by module rather than in one big cutover.

**Is $12,500 realistic, or a bait number?**
It's realistic for a standard first release with an India-based senior team — leads, pipeline, customer 360, basic reports, one integration. It is not realistic from a US agency, where the same scope starts around $40,000.

**What about maintenance costs?**
Plan 10–15% of the build cost per year for hosting, monitoring and small improvements. Unlike SaaS licences, this doesn't scale with seat count.
