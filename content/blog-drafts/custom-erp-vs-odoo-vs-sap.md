Every SME that outgrows spreadsheets faces the same three doors: a flexible open-source package (Odoo), an enterprise suite (SAP, Oracle, Dynamics), or [custom ERP development](https://qalbit.com/services/erp-development/). Each is right for someone. Here's how to tell which one is right for you — including the five-year costs vendors prefer not to put side by side.

## The comparison at a glance

| | Odoo (and similar) | SAP B1 / Dynamics | Custom ERP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | $10K–$40K implementation | $60K–$250K+ | $20K–$75K phased |
| Recurring | Per-user licences + partner fees | Heavy licences + AMC | Hosting + ~10–15%/yr support |
| Fits standard processes | Very well | Very well | N/A – built to yours |
| Fits *non-standard* processes | Customisation gets expensive fast | Consultant-heavy, slow | Natively |
| Upgrade risk | Custom modules break on version upgrades | Vendor-controlled roadmap | You control the roadmap |
| Data & code ownership | Partial (open core, your instance) | Vendor's | Fully yours |
| Best for | Standard SMEs starting fast | Large enterprises, group companies | Operations-led SMEs & manufacturers whose process is their edge |

## When Odoo is the right call

Odoo earns its popularity. Choose it when:

- **Your processes are close to standard.** Wholesale with simple flows, basic manufacturing, standard accounting — Odoo's defaults cover it, and implementation partners are plentiful.
- **You need many modules on day one** and can accept how they work out of the box.
- **Budget is genuinely capped under ~$20K** and you have someone internal who'll own the system.

The trap to price honestly: **the 30% rule.** Once you're customising more than ~30% of how Odoo works — bespoke job-work flows, unusual pricing logic, multi-branch approvals — you're paying partner day-rates to fight the framework, and every version upgrade re-breaks your customisations. We regularly meet companies whose "cheap" Odoo instance has quietly consumed more than a custom build would have cost, and they still don't own it.

## When SAP-class suites are the right call

Genuinely large operations: multiple legal entities, group consolidation, hundreds of concurrent users, auditors who expect a recognised suite. If that's you, this article isn't — you need an enterprise selection process. For SMEs, though, SAP-class implementations are consultant-led marathons at $150K–$500K+, sized for problems most growing businesses don't have.

## When custom wins

Choose [custom ERP development](https://qalbit.com/services/erp-development/) when:

1. **Your process is your competitive advantage.** Job-work chains, batch tracking, multi-location stock rules, unusual costing — the workflows that make you better than competitors are exactly what packages flatten into their defaults.
2. **You've already failed with a package.** A surprising share of our ERP work replaces half-adopted Odoo/legacy installs where staff went back to Excel. The software must fit the floor, not the other way around.
3. **You want to keep your accounting tool.** Custom ERPs sync happily with Tally, Zoho Books or QuickBooks — your CA keeps their system; your operations get theirs. Packages want to *be* your accounting.
4. **Per-user pricing punishes your growth.** 40 warehouse, floor and sales users on package licences is a permanent tax. Custom has no seat meter.
5. **Ten-year ownership matters.** Your code, your data, your upgrade schedule. No forced migrations, no end-of-life announcements.

Full pricing detail is in our [ERP development cost guide](https://qalbit.com/blog/erp-development-cost-2026/) — phase one typically lands at $20,000–$42,000.

## The five-year math (25-user example)

- **Odoo with moderate customisation:** ~$25K implementation + ~$12K/yr licences & partner maintenance + one paid version migration ≈ **$95K–$120K**, ownership: partial.
- **SAP B1:** $100K+ implementation + heavy AMC ≈ **$200K+**, ownership: none.
- **Custom (phased):** $32K build + ~$5K/yr support ≈ **$50K–$55K**, ownership: total.

Custom's curve is front-loaded and then flattens; packages start low and never stop climbing. The crossover almost always lands in year two or three.

## FAQ

**Can we migrate from Odoo (or a legacy ERP) to custom later?**
Yes — masters, transactions and history migrate, and we usually run both systems in parallel until numbers reconcile. Migration effort is part of any honest custom quote.

**Isn't custom riskier than a proven package?**
The risk profile is different, not higher. Package risk is adoption failure and customisation debt; custom risk is scope discipline. Phased fixed-scope delivery — one module proven before the next is funded — is how the custom risk is managed.

**What stack does a custom ERP use?**
Ours: Laravel or NestJS backends, React frontends, PostgreSQL/MySQL with strict constraints, and integrations to accounting/e-invoicing systems. Boring, proven technology any future team can maintain.
