"How long will it take?" is really two questions: how long until launch, and where do the weeks actually go? Here's the week-by-week timeline we run on [MVP development services](https://qalbit.com/services/mvp-development/) engagements — plus the honest list of what causes the delays that stretch 8 weeks into 6 months.

## The short answer

A properly scoped MVP ships in **6–10 weeks** from kickoff:

| Phase | Duration | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Scoping sprint | 1 week | One-page spec, fixed budget, launch criteria |
| 2. Design the core journey | 1–2 weeks | Clickable designs, approved before code |
| 3. Build in weekly increments | 3–6 weeks | Working product, demoed every Friday |
| 4. Launch & instrument | 1 week | Live MVP with analytics and error tracking |

Mobile adds 1–2 weeks (store review, device testing). Marketplaces add 2–4 weeks (two user sides). Anything quoted under 4 weeks is a prototype wearing an MVP costume; anything over 16 weeks is a full product wearing one.

## Week by week

**Week 1 — Scoping sprint.** The most valuable week of the project. We identify your riskiest assumption ("restaurants will pay for X", "users will connect their account"), then cut every feature that doesn't test it. Output: a one-page spec, fixed cost (see our [MVP cost guide](https://qalbit.com/blog/mvp-development-cost-2026/)), and measurable launch criteria. Founders who skip this week buy it back later at 5× the price in change requests.

**Weeks 2–3 — Design.** The core user journey designed end to end and clicked through with you *before* code. Proven UI patterns everywhere except the moments users judge you (onboarding, the "aha" screen). Design approval is the last cheap change point — everything after this costs engineering time.

**Weeks 3–8 — Build.** Overlapping design by design intent: backend starts while final screens are polished. You see a demo every Friday and course-correct in days, not months. Sequence: auth and skeleton first, core journey second, payments and admin third. The discipline that keeps timelines: new ideas go to the *post-launch* backlog, not the sprint.

**Weeks 8–9 — Launch and instrument.** Production deploy, analytics events mapped to your launch criteria, error tracking, and your first users onboarded — often manually, which is fine and even preferable. Launch is not the finish line; it's the start of the learning loop the whole MVP exists to feed.

## What actually causes delays

After a hundred-plus first releases, delays come from a short list — none of them typing speed:

1. **Slow decisions.** A 48-hour answer cadence on design and copy questions keeps the schedule; a two-week cadence doubles it. Assign one decision-maker.
2. **Scope creep mid-build.** "While you're in there, can we add…" — every yes moves the date. The scoping sprint exists so these become post-launch backlog items, not sprint items.
3. **Third-party surprises.** KYC providers, bank APIs and app-store review each carry external clocks. We front-load risky integrations into week 3–4 so surprises surface early.
4. **Content and assets.** Product copy, legal pages, seed data — the classic silent blocker. We flag every asset dependency in week 1 with an owner and a date.
5. **The 'one more polish' loop.** Perfection before evidence is procrastination with a design tool. Ship at credible, not flawless; users will tell you what to polish.

## What fits in 6–10 weeks (and what doesn't)

**Fits comfortably:** one core journey done well, auth and roles, Stripe/Razorpay billing, an admin panel, dashboards-lite, transactional email, analytics. That's a fundable, chargeable product.

**Doesn't fit (and shouldn't):** native iOS *and* Android *and* web simultaneously, chat + social + gamification, multi-language everything, AI features beyond one focused use case. These are phase-two candidates — funded by the evidence your MVP produces.

## FAQ

**Can you really launch in 6 weeks?**
Yes, when the scope is genuinely lean (one journey, web-first) and decisions come fast. Ten weeks is the honest median for a standard MVP with billing and admin.

**Does a fixed timeline mean rushed quality?**
No — it means fixed *scope*. Code quality, tests on critical paths and clean architecture are non-negotiable because the MVP that wins must survive success without a rewrite.

**What happens after launch?**
Two-week iteration sprints driven by real usage data against your launch criteria. If the metrics say "grow", the same team scales the product; if they say "pivot", you've spent weeks and thousands finding out — not quarters and hundreds of thousands.
