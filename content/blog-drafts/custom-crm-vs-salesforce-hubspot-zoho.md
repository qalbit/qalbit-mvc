## When off-the-shelf is the right answer

We tell roughly a third of the companies who contact us to stay on their current CRM. Buy (or keep) SaaS when:

- **Your sales process is standard.** Lead → call → proposal → close, with no unusual steps. Salesforce and HubSpot have refined this flow for two decades.
- **You have fewer than ~10 seats and no integration pain.** The licence math doesn't hurt yet.
- **You need a CRM next week.** No custom build beats a same-day signup when speed is everything.
- **Your team already lives in an ecosystem.** If marketing runs on HubSpot content tools, splitting the stack has real costs.

## When custom wins

Build when one or more of these is true:

1. **Your workflow doesn't fit their model.** Real-estate site visits and inventory, healthcare patient journeys, fintech KYC steps, project-based selling, multi-branch approval chains — if you're maintaining spreadsheets *next to* your CRM, the CRM has already failed.
2. **Licence bills grow faster than your team.** Per-seat pricing punishes growth. At 25–50 seats, three years of licences typically exceeds the entire cost of building. Our [custom CRM cost guide](https://qalbit.com/blog/crm-development-cost-2026/) has the full math.
3. **Integrations are the point.** If the CRM must talk to your ERP, Tally/QuickBooks, telephony, WhatsApp and product database, connector marketplaces get expensive and brittle. Custom integrations are built once, exactly for your stack.
4. **You're paying for 300 features and using 12.** Feature bloat isn't neutral — it kills adoption. Sales teams use custom CRMs *because* there's nothing on screen that isn't theirs.
5. **Data ownership matters.** Regulated industries, exit plans, AI plans — owning your customer data outright keeps every option open.

## The three numbers to compare (not two)

Most build-vs-buy analyses compare licence fees against build cost. Add the third number: **the cost of workarounds** — the admin hours spent re-keying data between systems, the deals lost to missed follow-ups the tool couldn't model, the reporting done in Excel because the CRM can't. For most companies we audit, workaround cost exceeds licence cost. Custom development eliminates both.

## The hybrid path nobody mentions

You don't have to rip out Zoho on day one. Common phased route we build:

1. **Keep the SaaS CRM, build the missing piece** — e.g. a quoting portal or field-visit app that syncs with it.
2. **Prove the custom module**, measure adoption.
3. **Replace the core when the licence renewal comes up**, migrating history into the custom system.

Lower risk, and the licence renewal date creates a natural deadline.

## FAQ

**Can a custom CRM really match Salesforce's features?**
It shouldn't try. Salesforce serves every industry; your CRM serves one — yours. Matching the 12 features your team uses takes 10–16 weeks; skipping the other 288 is the benefit, not the gap.

**What happens to our data if we build and it goes wrong?**
Your data lives in your database from day one. That's the structural difference: the worst-case custom scenario still leaves you owning everything.

**Can we migrate our Salesforce/Zoho history into a custom CRM?**
Yes — contacts, accounts, deals and (in most cases) activity history export cleanly. Migration and deduplication are part of any serious build quote.
