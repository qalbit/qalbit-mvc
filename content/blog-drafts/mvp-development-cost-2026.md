## What an MVP budget actually buys

A credible MVP is not a hackathon demo. A standard $8K–$18K web MVP includes:

- **Scoping sprint** — defining the riskiest assumption and cutting everything that doesn't test it (this is where most of the cost savings happen)
- **UX design for the core journey** — polished where users judge you, simplified everywhere else
- **The build**: authentication, the core workflow, payments if you charge from day one, and a basic admin panel
- **Launch infrastructure** — production + staging environments, CI/CD, error tracking
- **Analytics wired from day one** — so week one produces evidence, not anecdotes

## The five decisions that move your number

1. **Web first vs mobile first.** Web MVPs are 30–40% cheaper, skip app-store review, and iterate daily. Go mobile-first only if the idea genuinely lives on the phone (camera, GPS, push-driven habits). Many of our founders launch web, add a Flutter app after validation.
2. **One core journey vs three.** Every additional user journey (buyer + seller + admin vs just buyer) adds 30–50%. Marketplaces cost more precisely because they're three products pretending to be one — seed the supply side manually at first.
3. **Custom design vs proven patterns.** Investors don't fund gradients; they fund traction. Proven UI patterns save 10–15% and, honestly, usually test better.
4. **Integrations at launch.** Stripe/Razorpay is standard. But each additional integration (maps, KYC, messaging, calendars) adds cost — push any integration that doesn't test the core assumption to phase two.
5. **Who builds it.** Freelancer ($3K–$8K, single point of failure, you're the PM), local agency ($40K+), or a senior offshore product team — the middle path most funded pre-seed founders take.

## The two classic ways founders burn runway

**Overbuilding.** The $60K "MVP" with six features, chat, and an admin analytics suite — launched to discover users only wanted one feature. The fix is brutal scoping: if a feature doesn't test your riskiest assumption, it waits. (Our scoping sprint exists to have this fight before code is written.)

**Underbuying.** The $3K prototype that collapses at the first real users — no error tracking, no analytics, unmaintainable code a future CTO refuses to touch. You'll pay for the rebuild *and* have lost the learning window. An MVP must be small, not disposable.

The budget sweet spot buys the **smallest product that produces trustworthy evidence** — polished core journey, instrumented from day one, on an architecture that survives success. For how that maps week by week, see our [MVP timeline guide](https://qalbit.com/blog/mvp-development-timeline/).

## FAQ

**Can I build an MVP for under $5,000?**
Sometimes — if the test is genuinely tiny (a landing page + waitlist + concierge backend can cost under $2K and is often the *right* first test). A functioning product with auth and payments under $5K generally means corners you'll pay to un-cut later.

**Fixed price or hourly for an MVP?**
Fixed. Your runway math needs a number, and fixed-scope forces the scoping discipline that makes MVPs work. We quote a fixed cost and launch date after one scoping call.

**What ongoing costs come after launch?**
Hosting ($50–$300/month at MVP scale) plus iteration sprints as user feedback arrives. Budget roughly 15–25% of build cost for the first three post-launch months — the learning phase is the point of the whole exercise.

**Do you sign NDAs? Who owns the code?**
Yes, and you do — repositories, designs and infrastructure are yours from day one, documented well enough for a future CTO to take over.
